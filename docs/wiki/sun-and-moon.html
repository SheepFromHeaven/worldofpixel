Sun, moon and seasons

Day and night are a place, not a moment. The map is a strip of the planet, its rows are latitudes and its columns a 240 degree span of longitude, so the sun is high and short-shadowed in the tropics, low and long at the poles, and the middle of the map is at noon while its edges are at dawn and dusk.

Source: src/sim/astro.ts, src/sim/calendar.ts, src/render/lit/lighting.ts, src/render/lit/skyGrid.ts

1The clock and the calendar

Time is clock (0 to 1, a day, 0.5 is noon at the middle) plus elapsed, every day ever run. A day lasts 1440 seconds at normal speed, 24 minutes, and the speed multiplies it. A year is 32 days and the moon turns through 8 phases in 8 days. A game begins at 0.3 of the year, 18 jun, which is summer in the north.

  1. Season phaseThe year as 0 to 1, from 1 march. The north's; the south is half a year on.
  2. Ecliptic longitudeWhere the sun is on its path. Spring equinox at 0.125.
  3. DeclinationFrom the axial tilt: the sun's latitude.
  4. Hour angleFrom local solar time: noon is straight overhead.
  5. BearingEast, south and up. North is the top of the screen.

Everything drawn by sun and moon follows from the two elevations: daylight, moonLight, gloom, the ambient, the fog and the cloud colours. The shadows follow the bearings. The 2D ground cannot be re-lit, so its baked relief is lit from the top-left whatever the sun does, and a grade pass lays the night over it.

4A strip of the planet

Local solar time is the map centre's clock plus an offset of up to a third of a day (dayFractionOffsetAtMapPosition: 240 degrees over 360). The renderer cannot take one sky, so it evaluates skyAt on a 64 by 48 grid of places, packs the results into a texture, and every pass reads its own place from it. These two pictures are that same function, drawn.

The strip's time readout is the local time under the camera. The day dial and the time-zone view (17 one-hour bands) both read the same offset. Weather takes its day swing from each cell's own local time, so a front crossing the map brings its own afternoon with it.

6The moon

The moon is a real body. Its illumination is 0.5 - 0.5 cos(2 pi phase) and its light is a smoothstep of its height times that, so it casts its own fainter shadows only when it is up, lit and the sun is not.

7Seasons are a hemisphere

The season is the north's. The south is half a year on (southernSeasonPhaseOf). The shaders find a place's latitude from its world y and blend between the two tables across plus or minus 8 degrees of the equator, so a prop at the equator picks the northern or southern table by its own hash and the equator is a patchwork of autumn and spring and not a seam. The sun does not care: its declination is the one global season.

Seasons are the quarters of the year, and the equinoxes and solstices sit in the middle of each quarter, not at its start. So the sun crosses the equator on 15 April and reaches its peak on 15 July, about three and a half weeks after Earth does, and the date labels follow that.

The year slider in the sky panel is a day of the year (17 jun), with the season in each hemisphere underneath. dateName turns phase into a date and starts the year on the first of March. See the ecosystem page for what a season does to each species.
