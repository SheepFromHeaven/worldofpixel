World generation

A world is built once from its seed, in one pass, and nothing about it is stored. The same seed always gives the same ground. This page follows the build in order and draws every stage for seed 20250214.

Source: src/sim/terrain/worldfield.ts, heightmap.ts, hydrology.ts, climate.ts

1The tile is the whole idea

One tile is one square metre, sixteen texels across. The world is 2048 by 1408 tiles with a 64 tile skirt of sea round it, 3.3 million tiles in all. Everything below is computed at one cell per tile: the height, the flow, the climate and the biome. There is no second resolution to resample to and no lattice on top, so the biome array answers for a tile with nothing in between.

2The pipeline

  1. Raise the landEight octaves of noise, a smooth shore falloff.
  2. Fill the seasPriority flood from the edge, lakes in every hollow.
  3. Send the rainWind carries humidity across land and over hills.
  4. Cut the riversFlow accumulation, then a ribbon for every strong cell.
  5. Raise the windsLatitude belt, altitude, fetch, ravines, shelter.
  6. Sow the groundClassify each tile, then wander the edges.

The generator yields after each stage. The loading screen draws what it has so far, which is why you watch the world being built. Four files in strict dependency order do the work and none reaches back up: heightmap.ts, hydrology.ts, climate.ts, then worldfield.ts assembling them.

3Raising the land

Height is fbm of the tile position: 8 octaves starting at a 108 tile wavelength, each half the size and half the weight of the last, stretched by a contrast of 1.9, less the shore falloff. The last two octaves are for the rivers. Steepest descent down a field that is smooth at the size of a cell has no small valley to find, so every river runs dead straight and the whole map drains in parallel lines. Dropping octaves is how that comes back.

The world bounds itself

The falloff is a smooth norm of the distance from the middle, and past the extent it keeps growing, so the terrain's own noise cannot make islands in the skirt. A falloff that merely saturates does not do this. BEYOND is derived from the margin and must stay derived: a constant for one drifts from the other the moment the world changes size.

4Sea, lakes and rivers

The sea is every cell below sea level that connects to the edge of the map. From the sea a priority flood grows inland, always taking the lowest unvisited cell next and giving each cell the higher of its own height and the level the flood reached it at, plus a hair. Wherever that raised surface stands above the real ground, the hollow is a lake. Then every cell drains to its steepest neighbour and the rain is added up along the way. A cell carrying more than RIVER_FLUX (800) is a river.

Three rules that look like details and are not.

Route the water down the steepest slope, not back the way the flood came: the flood reaches a cell from whichever neighbour got there first, and following that back makes radial lines.

A flat is drained by the ground under it, not by the fill on top. Fill a hollow and its surface is level to the last bit, so steepest descent runs dead straight out of the spill point and a basin becomes a star of rays. The tie is broken on the drowned bed.

Lakes evaporate and a dry basin is a sink. Without that a desert is stippled with blue. Drying and filling settle each other, so they are asked in turn, three rounds at most.

This seed has 22,637 river segments and 1,995 river cells. A river is a ribbon narrower than a tile, so it is drawn from its segments and never written into the biome array. The meander is a field in world units, not jitter per cell, so neighbouring cells lean the same way and the line bends instead of fraying.

5Temperature and rain

The climate works in real units, degrees and the rainfall units the biome table was written in, because a model in made-up units cannot be checked against anything. environmentAt is where it is normalised to 0 to 1 for the game.

Rain is a wind that blows across each row from the side the prevailing wind comes from, carrying humidity. Over the sea it picks more up. Over land it drops some every step, a lot where the ground climbs, and it cannot cross anything above 0.81 altitude. A second, weaker pass blows down the map from the top or the bottom edge, picked by the seed, at a third of the humidity. Moisture then blends each cell's rain with its neighbours and adds the water in rivers.

6Choosing a biome

classify takes height, temperature, moisture and whether a river runs there. Highland wins above 0.62 altitude, then tundra below -5 degrees, then desert, then marsh, and otherwise a lookup in a five by twenty-six matrix of wetness against cold.

Water is three biomes and they are what blocks: isWater covers ocean, lake and river. Do not test elevation < SEA_LEVEL, because a lake stands above sea level and a river runs over land.

7Where biomes meet

Classifying a smooth field gives borders that are the level set of that field: smooth, tidy, and obviously drawn by a computer. wanderEdges rewrites the array by reading each land tile's biome from a position offset by three octaves of noise, a long lobe, a mid wave and a fine one that reaches about a tile.

It is baked into the array rather than applied in the painter so that what you see and what the tile says are the same fact. A warp in the painter alone would colour a tile meadow while the inspector, reading the array, called it desert. Water is never warped in either direction, since the painter's shoreline comes from the water mask.

The ground's own state is then derived from this: how well things grow, and what grows. The weather reads the same field through a coarse map, one texel to 16 tiles: see weather.

Determinism. Insertion order everywhere and explicit tie-breaks. Nothing in this pass may use Math.random or the clock, and the biome table above is the first thing to change if someone does.
