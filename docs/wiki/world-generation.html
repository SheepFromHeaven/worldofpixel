World generation

A world is built once from its seed, in one pass, and nothing about it is stored. The same seed always gives the same ground. This page follows the build in order and draws every stage for seed 20250214.

Source: src/sim/terrain/worldfield.ts, heightmap.ts, hydrology.ts, climate.ts

1The tile is the whole idea

One tile is one square metre, sixteen texels across. The world is 2048 by 1408 tiles with a 64 tile skirt of sea round it, 3.3 million tiles in all. Everything below is computed at one cell per tile: the height, the flow, the climate and the biome. There is no second resolution to resample to and no lattice on top, so the biome array answers for a tile with nothing in between.

2The pipeline

  1. Raise the landEight octaves of noise, a smooth shore falloff, then cliffs, mesas, canyons and volcanoes.
  2. Fill the seasPriority flood from the edge, lakes in every hollow.
  3. Send the rainWind carries humidity across land and over hills.
  4. Cut the riversFlow accumulation, then a ribbon for every strong cell.
  5. Raise the windsLatitude belt, altitude, fetch, ravines, shelter.
  6. Sow the groundClassify each tile, then wander the edges.
  7. Lay the rockThe geology picks bare rock, karst, volcanic ground, salt and badlands, and how fertile each tile is.

The generator yields after each stage. The loading screen draws what it has so far, which is why you watch the world being built. Four files in strict dependency order do the work and none reaches back up: heightmap.ts, hydrology.ts, climate.ts, then worldfield.ts assembling them.

3Raising the land

Height is fbm of the tile position: 8 octaves starting at a 108 tile wavelength, each half the size and half the weight of the last, stretched by a contrast of 1.9, less the shore falloff. The last two octaves are for the rivers. Steepest descent down a field that is smooth at the size of a cell has no small valley to find, so every river runs dead straight and the whole map drains in parallel lines. Dropping octaves is how that comes back.

The world bounds itself

The falloff is a smooth norm of the distance from the middle, and past the extent it keeps growing, so the terrain's own noise cannot make islands in the skirt. A falloff that merely saturates does not do this. BEYOND is derived from the margin and must stay derived: a constant for one drifts from the other the moment the world changes size.

4Cliffs, mesas, canyons and volcanoes

Plain noise only makes hills, so the height is shaped before the sea is filled, first by plate tectonics (plates.ts) and then by landforms.ts. The plates give the large structure and the volcanoes follow them: an arc on the overriding side of a subduction, a line along a rift, a few elsewhere. Steep ground, where the height changes by more than 0.12 in four tiles, is bare rock whatever the climate says. On top of the plates, four local landforms, each living in its own part of the world: shore cliffs lift the land by 0.05 (about 190 m) within a few tiles of the waterline in the half of the coast that the cliff province covers, so the beach is a wall; mesas terrace the broad land into stacked plateaus with escarpments between; canyons cut a meandering trench of a fixed width, 0.08 deep, along a warped ridged noise, and run into the sea as fjords; volcanoes are a cone with a crater, gullied down the flanks and roughened, one at most per 640 tile cell and dropped near the world's edge so they are never in the skirt.

The provinces are slow value noise, so a character holds for hundreds of tiles. Everything keys on world position and the seed, so the landforms are the same however the field is reached. The hydrology needs no change: the lowest ground is the canyon floor, so a river finds it, a crater with a rim holds a lake, and a cliff coast just has no shelf.

5Sea, lakes and rivers

The sea is every cell below sea level that connects to the edge of the map. From the sea a priority flood grows inland, always taking the lowest unvisited cell next and giving each cell the higher of its own height and the level the flood reached it at, plus a hair. Wherever that raised surface stands above the real ground, the hollow is a lake. Then every cell drains to its steepest neighbour and the rain is added up along the way. A cell carrying more than RIVER_FLUX (400) is a river.

Three rules that look like details and are not.

Route the water down the steepest slope, not back the way the flood came: the flood reaches a cell from whichever neighbour got there first, and following that back makes radial lines.

A flat is drained by the ground under it, not by the fill on top. Fill a hollow and its surface is level to the last bit, so steepest descent runs dead straight out of the spill point and a basin becomes a star of rays. The tie is broken on the drowned bed.

Lakes evaporate and a dry basin is a sink. Without that a desert is stippled with blue. A closed basin holds only the water its inflow can feed: the inflow is half the rain that reaches it (RUNOFF_SHARE, the land keeps the rest), and every wet tile evaporates in proportion to its warmth. A lake that cannot cover its whole surface shrinks to its lowest tiles, the ones the inflow can keep wet, and is gone if it cannot keep even the lowest, and it never sits below sea level. That keeps lakes a few percent of the land at every size of world, and the great ones for the wet basins. Drying and filling settle each other, so they are asked in turn, three rounds at most.

This seed has 29,004 river segments and 3,198 river cells. A river is a ribbon narrower than a tile, so it is drawn from its segments and never written into the biome array. The meander is a field in world units, not jitter per cell, so neighbouring cells lean the same way and the line bends instead of fraying.

Banks and depth

Continents come from the plates, not from the noise. A plate is continental with a chance that depends on the seed (42% plus up to 32%, so one world is an archipelago and another nearly one landmass) and lifts the ground 0.08, an oceanic one sinks it 0.28, over a base that sits 0.08 above the sea, and the fractal noise only roughens that (its contrast is 1.25, it was 1.9 and drew the whole map as one blobby landmass). Plates are 320 tiles across with a 120 tile warp, the coast between a continental and an oceanic plate is a 34 tile shelf rather than a ruled edge, and the world's own edge only starts to pull the land down at 72% of the way out. The result is a few continents of different sizes with seas between them, peninsulas, and island chains where ocean plates meet. tools/continents.ts draws six seeds side by side.

Rain is recycled over land. Air that has rained out over land picks most of it up again from plants and soil, so the humidity of an air column falls over a length 1 / (1 - 0.55) times longer than the rain falls (RECYCLE in climate.ts, applied exactly inside fallout so it does not depend on the step). Continental interiors therefore stay green: about a third of the land is forest, a sixth meadow, and desert belongs to the dry latitudes and rain shadows.

Lakes erode. Before the water balance is asked (erodeLakes in hydrology.ts), every lake loses what its outlet would cut: 0.005 of height plus a quarter of its depth, so shallow lakes vanish and deep ones shrink to their core; a lake whose surface is within 0.02 of the sea opens to it and is gone; and no lake keeps more than 5000 tiles of surface, because a bigger one needs a basin the world does not have.

Lakes sit in basins the land makes. Lowland carries bowl-shaped sags (basinDepth in landforms.ts: a jittered site every 220 tiles, 40% of them with a basin 16 to 50 tiles in radius and 45 to 130 m deep, never taking the ground below the sea), so the flood has real hollows to fill, and a lake keeps what 70% of its inflow can feed against 0.5 per degree of warmth over its surface, which gives a few big lakes and many small ones, 3 to 5% of the land. Rivers start small. A channel counts from a flux of 400 at a quarter of a tile of half width and widens with the square root of its flux; a headwater that runs less than 12 tiles before it joins another, or the sea, is dropped.

Close rivers merge. Steep wet ground gives many channels that each carry just enough water to count as a river, side by side like the teeth of a comb. After the rivers are traced, an opening pass (openRivers in hydrology.ts) walks them from the biggest down: a river cell with a river at least 1.3 times its flux within 7 tiles, that is not its own downstream path, is given up, and what is left of its branch is drawn into that neighbour instead. Rivers of flux 8000 and over are never given up.

The painter does not stop at the waterline. Water is shaded by how deep it is: a river is pale at its edge and dark in its middle, and a bigger river is deeper overall, so a stream never gets as dark as a great river. A lake or the sea is lightened over the first tile or so off the shore, the shallows. Where a river runs through a standing-water cell the river's own shade wins, so the join has no seam.

The land beside the water changes with the depth too. A wet strip runs along the waterline, wider the further its size is from the middle: shallow streams leave a pale gravel bar, deep rivers a dark mud bank, lakes mud and the open sea a sand beach. Outside the strip the ground is darkened a little, damp, and fades back to the biome. The strip is drawn from the path ramps, so flat marks keep off it, and its edge wanders on a noise so it is never a ruled line.

6Temperature and rain

The climate works in real units, degrees and the rainfall units the biome table was written in, because a model in made-up units cannot be checked against anything. environmentAt is where it is normalised to 0 to 1 for the game.

Rain is a wind that blows across each row from the side the prevailing wind comes from, carrying humidity. Over the sea it picks more up. Over land it falls out in proportion to what it carries, over a reach of about 30 tiles with a floor of one rain unit a tile, and a lot where the ground climbs, and it cannot cross anything above 0.81 altitude. Each step is solved exactly (the fall-out is an exponential, then a steady floor), so the answer does not depend on how big a cell is, and the reach and the humidity grow with the width of the world so the same continent has the same climate at any size. Rain is made on its own, coarser grid for a world that is built in windows. A second, weaker pass blows down the map from the top or the bottom edge, picked by the seed, at a third of the humidity. Moisture then blends each cell's rain with its neighbours and adds the water in rivers.

7Choosing a biome

classify takes height, temperature, moisture and whether a river runs there. Highland wins above 0.62 altitude, then tundra below -5 degrees, then desert, then marsh, and otherwise a lookup in a five by twenty-six matrix of wetness against cold.

  • meadow
  • forest
  • highland
  • marsh
  • tundra
  • desert

Water is three biomes and they are what blocks: isWater covers ocean, lake and river. Do not test elevation < SEA_LEVEL, because a lake stands above sea level and a river runs over land.

8Where biomes meet

Classifying a smooth field gives borders that are the level set of that field: smooth, tidy, and obviously drawn by a computer. wanderEdges rewrites the array by reading each land tile's biome from a position offset by three octaves of noise, a long lobe, a mid wave and a fine one that reaches about a tile.

It is baked into the array rather than applied in the painter so that what you see and what the tile says are the same fact. A warp in the painter alone would colour a tile meadow while the inspector, reading the array, called it desert. Water is never warped in either direction, since the painter's shoreline comes from the water mask.

9What the rock does to the surface

The climate alone paints a world of six grounds. Five more come from what is under them, and they are real biomes, so the painter, the plants and the inspector all see them through the one array. The last stage of the build (terrain/surface.ts) builds the geology of the world (see geology) while the field is still being made, reads it at four tile resolution blended smoothly to the tile, and rewrites field.biome. The climate's own answer is kept as field.climate, because the geology's soil depends on it and must never depend on its own result.

How well things grow

The same pass writes field.fertility, a byte with 200 meaning an ordinary loam: the soil depth (a thin soil is worth less, down to 60%), the kind of soil (sand 0.5, clay 0.85), what the bedrock weathers into (volcanic ash and basalt soils are rich, granite, quartzite and limestone are poor, gypsum and salt very poor) and the biome itself (bare rock 0.12, salt 0.03, fresh lava and ash 0.3, karst 0.45, badlands 0.35). It multiplies the eco score, so the forms and densities that were already chosen by eco follow the soil: a forest thins on a granite ridge and a meadow runs over a basalt plain. The plants themselves grow at 0.25 + 0.75 * fertility of their climate vigour, and herbs spread by the same factor.

Determinism. Insertion order everywhere and explicit tie-breaks. Nothing in this pass may use Math.random or the clock, and the biome table above is the first thing to change if someone does.
