Reptiles

Reptiles are the first animals here big enough to be a shape rather than a speck: a green anaconda is five metres, eighty texels, a sea turtle a metre across, a saltwater crocodile five and a half. They are populations of the food web, not a map of habitat: every patch holds eggs, hatchlings, juveniles and adults of each species, they eat insects, frogs, fish, each other and (for tortoises, iguanas and sea turtles) plants, and a huge lizard is rare because it needs a great deal of food and ground. This page is what a species is like, how a body is drawn and how it behaves when you are near it. 37 species are in (13 snakes and legless lizards, 11 lizards, 2 chameleons, 6 turtles and tortoises, 5 crocodilians: an alligator, the Nile, saltwater and gharial crocodiles and a caiman) and a new one is a row here and a line of biology in sim/fauna/life.ts.

2Habitat, the food web, and what is drawn

  1. HabitatFour numbers per tile: water, cover, warm, bank. Land, fresh water and the sea each fill them differently, so one table of tastes serves all three media.
  2. RealmA species may live on land, in fresh water, in the sea, or any mix. A patch is suitable for it by how much of its realm and taste the patch has, and a hop or crawl to a spot on the wrong medium falls back to home.
  3. PopulationThe food web keeps the eggs, hatchlings, juveniles and adults of each species in each patch: how many, how well fed, how many young are coming. Big hunters are few because they need so much; the numbers settle on their own. See the food web page.
  4. SlotsThe ground is cut into 8 m cells with 70 slots in each, the species' slot counts laid end to end (one for the big ones, three for the small). A slot wakes when its fixed random number is under the density the patch holds, spread over the places the species likes and scaled by the hour, the cold, the rain and snow.
  5. SizeEach awake slot is a hatchling at a fifth of adult length, a juvenile at half or an adult, in the proportion the patch holds them. Turtles and crocodilians that have eggs in the patch also show a nest on the bank: a dark mound of vegetation for a crocodile, a scrape of sand for a turtle, with pale eggs.
  6. MotionWhere an awake animal is, which way it faces, how its body is bent and what its legs are doing are functions of the clock and the slot. Nothing about one animal is stored.

A click is not a hunt. What a click does (below) is only seen: the animal runs and comes back. The food web has no individual animals, so a reptile that flees is not one that has been eaten or fed; the patch holds the count.

3The species

The four tastes are, in order, water (the quality of the water tile it is in), cover, warm (open ground to bask on, or shallows) and bank. A body is drawn at 3 texels plus 16 per metre of length, so it is almost life size: a 5 m python is 83 texels, a 13 cm gecko five. How common each is, is not a number here: it is what the food web finds.

4Habitat: four maps of the world

Built by reptileHabitatAt into an RGBA texture, a band of sixteen rows a frame nearest the camera first, by the same HabitatTexture as the insects and amphibians, with no smoothing. A water tile carries its quality in the first channel, which is all the shader needs to know the medium: zero is land, up to 0.1 is sea, above is fresh water.

5The clock: when each species is out

Snow cover over 10% sends everything under, melting away by 40%.

6Where they could live on this seed

Suitability times the climate for six species on this seed, from abundance, with nobody eating anybody: the ground a species can use. The animals actually kept there, and how few big hunters that is, are on the food web page.

7How each form moves and looks

  • Snakes are a chain of 22 capsules whose heading follows a sine wave along the body, and the wave is fixed in the ground while the head moves through it, so the body follows its own track (the phase is the distance travelled). At rest the body relaxes into an S or, for the big boas, a coil, chosen by the animal's own number. Widths taper over the last third. A snake flicks a forked tongue, a cobra spreads its hood and shows pale marks when it stops and rears, a rattlesnake shakes a tail of three pale beads, a sidewinder travels at an angle to its body, and a slow worm is blunt-tailed. Patterns are read along the body: stripe, bands (coral snakes get the red, black, yellow, black ring order), diamonds, zigzag, blotches.
  • Lizards have a head, torso and a tapered tail that swings, four legs in a diagonal gait whose stride matches the ground covered, and a wobble down the spine. Traits add a crest (iguanas, basilisks), a spiny beard (bearded dragon), spines at the outline (thorny devil), a forked flick (komodo, monitors) or a broad blue one (the blue-tongued skink).
  • Chameleons sit at canopy height, over the plants, with a casque, eyes on turrets, a tail rolled into a spiral and a slow rocking walk. They drift between their two colours over a few minutes and blend toward the ground colour under them by reading the painted ground buffer.
  • Turtles have a shell with scutes (a Voronoi of plates with seams, some plates picking up the accent colour, a rim band), a head that stretches out as it moves, and legs. Tortoises are domed with stumpy legs, pond turtles flatter and webbed, and the sea turtle is heart-shaped with flippers that beat as it swims.
  • Crocodilians are long, with a tapered snout, raised eyes, a pale osteoderm ridge down the back and a tail that sways side to side. In water the legs are tucked and the body is drawn half through the surface.
  • In water: an animal whose home is on water is dithered 40% transparent and tinted dark except its head, eyes and, for crocodiles, its back; a turtle's shell stays whole. A swimming animal sheds ripple rings behind it.
  • Startled: a click throws a ripple (LitLayer.throwRipple) and every reptile inside its flee distance runs, a moment apart, at 3.5 times its pace to a point on the far side. Species that bolt for water (iguanas, basilisks, monitors, turtles, crocodilians) look for a wet spot among nine directions and make for it, and land with a ring. Giant tortoises and snapping turtles stand their ground. The last 16 clicks are remembered and the displacement eases back over a couple of minutes.
  • Shadows: any animal over 5 texels long on land casts a small offset shadow, drawn first, never over water.

Every animal is a distance field rasterised on the world's own texel grid, not a sprite, facing one of 32 headings, lit from the top-left in steps with an inked edge, and faded in and out through the Bayer dither. Ground animals are drawn under the plants and the chameleon and tree boa over them. Nothing is drawn below strip zoom 14.
