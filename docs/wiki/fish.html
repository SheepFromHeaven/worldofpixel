Fish

Fish are not placed one at a time. The unit is the shoal: a position, a heading, a species and a headcount. A world holds a few thousand of them, each one swims where its species can live, eats what it can catch, grows toward what the water can feed and splits when it gets too big. 10 species live in rivers, lakes and the sea. On screen each shoal is drawn as a handful of dark shadows moving under the surface.

Source: src/sim/fish/, src/render/lit/fishPass.ts, src/render/lit/fishInstances.ts, src/render/lit/shaders/fish.vert

2How a shoal lives

  1. WaterEvery water tile knows its depth, its distance from land, its current and the water plants beside it (World.depth, shore, flow, aqua).
  2. FitnessA species scores a tile from 0 to 1: temperature, depth, current and shore distance multiply, plants it likes raise it.
  3. SwimEvery second or so a shoal looks at nine directions a few tiles ahead and eases round toward the best one at under half a radian a second, over a spread of about plus or minus one radian, so a shoal swims in long gentle arcs rather than turning on the spot. It never enters a tile it cannot live in. How fast it swims depends on the local time: each species has an activity by day, by night and a bonus at dusk and dawn (activityOf, never below 0.1), minnows and perch rest at night while carp, cod, flatfish and herring are most active in the dark and pike and trout peak at dusk, and a resting shoal drifts slowly, darts less, weaves less and its shadow is fainter.
  4. EatA predator takes a share of the prey shoals within 14 tiles. A shoal that cannot find food grows slower, and starves below a threshold.
  5. GrowLogistic growth toward a carrying capacity of school size × fitness of the tile it sits on. Over 1.7 times a full school it splits in two.
  6. DrawnSeen from above, a shoal is a few dark fish shapes under the surface, or a dithered patch when you are far out.

Data. A shoal is a row of typed arrays (Shoals: position, heading, species, headcount, depth) so the worker can send the whole fleet to the page cheaply. The page interpolates between updates. Fish are saved nowhere yet, so they are regenerated from the seed when a game loads.

Rare things stay rare. Each species has a common weight and a start only places a shoal where fitness × commonness beats a roll. Tuna and pike are a hundredth as many as herring and minnow, because a predator that outweighs its prey eats them out of the water. A few strays also arrive from nowhere every ecology step (recolonise) so a species that dies out in one lake can come back.

4Where each species can live

Water temperature is 70% the tile's annual mean and 30% the air at the moment, so a hard winter or a hot summer moves the fish. Shore distance matters to open-water species: tuna stay at least a few tiles off the coast.

5When each species is active

Activity (activityOf) is the species' night value blended into its day value by the local daylight, plus dusk × 4 × daylight × (1 − daylight), which peaks in the half-light, and never falls below 0.1. The daylight is that of the shoal's own place on the map (the same per-place light the insects and people use), refreshed each time it re-aims. It scales how far a shoal drifts, and, when drawn, how often its fish dart, how wide they weave and how strong their shadow is, so a resting shoal hangs almost still and faint while a night feeder is lively in the dark.

7Predators, prey and growth

A predator is hungry in proportion to its mass and eats at most 8% of any one prey shoal per step, so a shoal is cropped, never wiped out in a step. What it cannot catch it makes up from insects and plankton down to half its need (FORAGE_FLOOR), so the pyramid of a few big fish over a lot of small ones holds. Below 40% fed a predator starves.

An ecology step runs every 0.05 game days, about a minute and a quarter at 1x. Swimming runs every frame.

8Eggs, fry, and the rest of the web

A shoal also carries eggs and fry. When the water is in the species' spawning range the shoal lays eggs, in proportion to the growth it would have had, with enough over to cover what is lost on the way (about a fifth of eggs become adults when nothing special eats them). Eggs hatch in a fraction of a generation, fry grow up over a longer one and then join the adults, and fry are a small prey (a twentieth of an adult's mass) that the hunting fish take along with the shoal. Nothing in a shoal that has no eggs left can be replaced, so a shoal overfished in one step recovers on a slower clock than before.

Fish are now part of the same food web as the insects, amphibians and reptiles. Perch, trout and minnows take tadpoles, frog spawn and insect larvae from the water of their patch, carp take larvae and spawn (never more than a quarter of what is there in a step, so a pond is never emptied), and a lizard, snake, turtle or crocodile that eats fish takes a share of the shoals of its patch. The visible shoals are a sample of the fish in the water around them, so what a reptile eats is scaled up by 25 from what a shoal then loses. A shoal's food shortage can be the lack of insects, and insects are fewer where the frogs are many.

9How they are drawn

Fish glide and dart. A shoal drifts at GLIDE (0.45) of the species' cruise speed, so most of the time fish hang in the water. On top of that each fish has its own darts: in every period a fish rolls chance to bolt in a random direction for several body lengths in a fraction of a second, then eases back toward the shoal over a few seconds, and its tail beats harder while it does. A minnow darts every few seconds, a carp rarely and lazily, a pike mostly lies still and a tuna makes long runs. Everything is a function of the clock, so the page needs no per-fish state, and heading is the direction a fish is actually going.

The fish pass (FishPass) runs after the water pass and before the grade pass, so night darkens the shadows with everything else. The CPU turns every shoal in view into one to fourteen fish (fishInstances): each fish weaving on its own phase around the shoal's centre and turned along its own direction of travel, so a shoal that turns never swings sideways. Far out (strip zoom under 4.5) a shoal is one dithered blob instead. The fish shape is a signed distance in the fragment shader, a long, slim fish seen from above (a tapered body about a fifth as wide as it is long, a narrow wrist and a forked tail fin) whose whole body undulates with a travelling wave (stronger toward the tail and with speed) and which bends into a turn (each fish faces along its path over the half second either side of now, and its body is drawn as an arc whose curvature is how far that path turns divided by the distance it covers while turning, so a fish bows round a corner, head and tail both swinging to the inside, instead of spinning rigidly on the spot), so there is no sprite sheet; its edge is not drawn hard but fades over a few hundredths of a body width through the Bayer dither (density 0.8 at the core, 0 a little outside the outline), so the shadow reads as something seen through water.

A fish is only drawn on pixels the ground painter coloured as water, so a shoal near a shore never puts a shadow on the bank. Deep water fades the shadow (to 20% at 20 m), the day fades it (30% at night), and ice over a frozen lake hides it.
