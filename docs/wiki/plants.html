Plants

A tile holds up to three plants, one in each layer (canopy, understorey, herb), and each has a species, an age and a height. Nothing is rolled once and left: a plant grows along a logistic curve fitted to how many years the real species takes to reach 90% of its height, shades the ones beside it, thirsts, dies and seeds its neighbours. 45 species are catalogued, from tundra sedge to rainforest kapok.

Source: src/sim/plants/, src/pixel/ground/flora.ts, src/pixel/ground/plants.ts

1The model

  1. NicheFive trapezoids (temperature, rain, altitude, salt spray, nearness to fresh water) multiply into a fitness from 0 to 1.
  2. GrowthHeight follows a logistic curve whose rate comes from years to 90% of full height, scaled by vigour.
  3. CompetitionLight is taken by taller neighbours in a 5 by 5 patch. Water is a budget the stand shares.
  4. SeedA seedling can only appear where a mature parent is within its dispersal reach, by gravity, wind, animal or water.
  5. DeathA background hazard by age and size, plus drought, shade, felling and storms. A dead tree may stand as a snag, then rot.
  6. FruitMature plants bear a crop in kilocalories in a ripe window of the year. People forage it.

The data is flat arrays. Per tile, per layer: species (u8), age in game days (u16) and height (u8, stored squared so small plants keep resolution). world.cover is derived from them. Only tiles a person or a storm has changed are saved; natural drift is regenerated from the seed on load.

Time. A game year is 32 game days, about twelve and a half real hours at 1x, and it is the unit the calendar, fruit and seasons run in. Plants live on a faster clock: GROWTH_SPEED is 100, so a plant ages and grows 100 plant years for every game year, one plant year in about 6 real minutes at 1x. The figures on this page (years to 90%, lifespan) are plant years. A birch is full grown after about 40 plant years, around four real hours at 1x; an oak after about twelve, a bramble in half an hour. Ripe windows are not sped up.

3Growth in plant years

The sprite follows the height: sapling, young, low, mid and high are 15%, 35%, 60%, 100% and above of the species height, and each is drawn at its own scale, shadow length and draw order. Trees, bushes and the plant marks (grasses, reeds, flowers, ferns, mushrooms, cacti) are drawn from the side, so a crown sits on its trunk and a reed or a blade of grass sways from its foot; their top-down sprite is kept only to cast the shadow.

4Where each species can live

Altitude, salt and damp work the same way. Salt spray is exp(-distance to ocean / 5), so coconut, sea buckthorn and dune grass hold the first few tiles of coast. Damp is how near fresh water is, which is what puts willow and sedge on banks.

Climates are grouped into 12 zones (polar desert, tundra, alpine, boreal forest, steppe, temperate forest, temperate rainforest, mediterranean scrub, hot desert, savanna, tropical seasonal forest, rainforest) by temperature, rain and altitude. The generator uses the zone to choose how closed the stand is and which form a tile wants.

6Light, water and shade

Vigour is sqrt(fitness) × min(light response, water factor). Light falls with the height-weighted crown cover of taller neighbours in a 5 by 5 patch, and each species has a shade tolerance: a fern is happy under a canopy, a birch is not. Water is a carrying capacity: each tile supports a biomass set by rain and nearness to water, and every plant in the layer takes a share of it by size and thirst. Below the capacity everything is fine. Above it the thirstiest and smallest take a drought hazard. This is what stops scrub from filling a desert and thins a wood on dry ground.

A seedling is only allowed where the generator's own answer for the tile wants that form (wants), so a world relaxes back toward the pattern it was generated as and a hole in a wood fills where the trees stood.

7Seeds come from parents

Each species disperses by one of four kernels with a reach in tiles. Gravity drops seed at the foot of the parent (banana, bamboo). Wind carries it downwind, biased by the prevailing wind of the row's latitude band (birch, pine, spruce). Animals carry fruit farther (oak acorns, apples, figs). Water moves it along rivers and coasts (willow, coconut). A tile can only be seeded when a parent of at least its matureAge stands within reach, and the number of seedlings scales with the parent's recruits and size.

8Fruit and foraging

A mature plant carries a crop that scales with its size and vigour, in kilocalories, ripe in a window of the local year (the southern hemisphere is half a year on). An idle hungry person walks to the nearest ripe crop within reach and eats up to a stomach (650 kcal) at a time. A crop picked is gone until next year. One hunger unit is 6000 kcal.

9Every species

17 trees, 14 bushes, 4 boulders and 29 ground marks. Sprites are the baked albedo, one per storey for the big ones (low, mid and high stand at different heights and cast different shadows).

Trees show their niche and where the kind actually stands on this seed. Bushes and boulders have two faces: the share bars say how likely this species is to be the one picked when a tile has that form, and the curve multiplies it by the biome's density curve to give the share of all tiles the species holds as eco rises. A line per biome it grows in. Marks are not rolled by tile. Each has a density a tile in the biome it belongs to, multiplied by a patch field (shown as a map, dark where it grows).

                              10Marks grow in patches

                              A flat mark's density in a tile is its weight times a patch field (patchiness), then rounded stochastically: a density of 0.4 puts one mark in four tiles. The field is one or more layers of value noise, each with a scale in tiles and a cover, multiplied together and scaled up to keep the mean. Flowers share one big field and each colour has its own inside it. Ferns and mushrooms clump tighter. Cacti and boulders get a wide, loose field and half the density, so they stand apart.

                              The noise is seeded by the world and is a pure function of the tile, so a patch is the same wherever you come at it from.
