Amphibians

Amphibians live a double life, so the world keeps them in two places. Adults are on the land near fresh water and come out for damp, night and warmth; eggs and tadpoles are in the calm water of the patch. Where they are and how many is the food web: each patch holds eggs, tadpoles, froglets and adults of every species, they eat the insects and are eaten by snakes, lizards, turtles, crocodilians and fish, and breed in proportion to how well they eat. This page is how those numbers are drawn and what a species likes. 10 species are in, and a new one is a row here and a line of biology in sim/fauna/life.ts.

2Habitat, the food web, and what is drawn

  1. HabitatFour numbers per tile, built from the world: pond, shelter, damp, bank. A texture, read with no smoothing, so a tile is either water or land. Averaged over a patch it says how suitable the patch is, and for the water-bound stages an exact count of the calm pond tiles says how much room eggs and tadpoles have.
  2. PopulationThe food web keeps eggs, tadpoles, froglets and adults of every species in every patch, and moves them with feeding, breeding, growth, hunger, predators and the weather. See the food web page.
  3. SlotsThe ground is cut into 6 m cells with 83 slots in each. Some are adults, some are young (spawn or tadpoles). A slot wakes when its fixed random number is below the density the patch holds, spread over the places the species likes and scaled by the hour, the weather and the season: an animal underground, in the cold or in the rain is not seen.
  4. SizeA froglet is drawn at half size and an adult at full size, picked slot by slot in the proportion the patch holds them, so a pond shows tadpoles, froglets and frogs together and a population that has just bred has more small ones.
  5. MotionWhere an awake slot is and which way it faces is a function of the clock and the slot. Nothing about one animal is stored; the stored thing is the count in the patch.

What a click does. A click on water still scares every hopper within a couple of metres, and nothing of it is kept in the sim: the animal returns over a couple of minutes. What the animals eat and are eaten by is all in the patch.

3The species

The first four numbers are tastes, the rest are limits. Needs wet is how much the animal waits for rain or damp ground: a salamander is almost never seen dry, a pool frog hardly cares, and a spadefoot toad (1.0) appears only when it is wet. A body is drawn at 3.2 texels plus 30 texels per metre of length, so even a 2.5 cm dart frog is a readable four texels, which is the same exaggeration the items and fish make.

4Habitat: four maps of the world

Built by amphibianHabitatAt into an RGBA texture, a band of sixteen rows a frame nearest the camera first, the same way as the insect habitat (both use HabitatTexture). A water tile carries only its pond quality, with a tiny floor so the shader can tell water of any kind from land; the ocean carries only the floor, which no amphibian likes.

5The clock: when each species is out

Snow cover over 10% sends everything under, melting away by 40%.

6Spawn, tadpoles, froglets, frogs

Seven species leave spawn you can see. It is the egg stock of the patch, so it appears in the water where adults of the patch are fed well enough to breed, in the weeks the water is warm enough for that species (water in the table is the temperature range), and it is eaten by newts, by fish (perch, minnows, carp take spawn) and by anything that eats eggs. It hatches with warmth into tadpoles, the hatchling stock: two dark texels and a flicking tail, four to a group, drifting and darting, eaten by fish, dragonfly larvae and snakes, and fed on the algae of the pond, so a pond with no plants raises few. They grow at the pace they feed into froglets, drawn half size on the bank, and then into adults.

Frog spawn is a clump of pale jelly texels with dark nuclei, toad spawn a double wavy string, both drawn only on pixels the painter coloured as water. Frogs and toads that are breeding also call after dark: a male sits still and a pale throat sac swells four times a second in bursts.

7Where they could live on this seed

Suitability times the climate for four species on this seed, from abundance, without anyone eating anyone: the ground a species can use. The animals actually kept there are on the food web page, and are always fewer where predators are.

8How each one moves and looks

  • Hop (frogs, tree frogs, dart frogs, spadefoots): sit for several seconds (a tree frog for half a minute), then one hop of up to about two metres in 0.38 s. In the air the body is lifted up the screen, the legs stretch out behind it and a small shadow stays on the ground. Hops are between spots near home, and a spot on the wrong medium (water for a land frog, land for a floating one) is replaced by home, so a frog never hops into a lake by itself.
  • Walk (toads, salamanders, newts): a slow pause and walk at about 0.2 m/s, the legs swinging in pairs, and for a salamander a wave running down the body and tail.
  • Afloat: a species that likes the pond (pool frog, newt) can have its home on the water, where it floats with the legs trailing and glides rather than hops.
  • Startled: a click on water throws a ripple, and every frog within 2 m hops 0.9 to 1.8 m away, a moment apart, a tenth of a second at most, and lands with a ring where that is water. The last 16 clicks are remembered, as for the water striders, and the displacement eases back over a couple of minutes.

An adult is a distance field rasterised at the world's own texel grid, not a sprite: a body ellipse and a head, two eyes, hind legs folded or stretched, front legs, and a tail for a salamander, built from creature in amphibians.frag. It faces one of sixteen headings, is lit in five steps from the top-left like everything else, and gets a darker edge texel. Skin is a base colour with an accent scattered by a hash of the texel: single dots for spotted, larger blotches, or a pale stripe down the back. Frogs and toads are sorted with the props by the height of their hop, so a plant covers them; tree frogs are held at canopy height, over the props, and have no shadow. Nothing is drawn below strip zoom 24.
