Idles

An idle is a short thing an animal does while it stands still: a brown bear rising on its hind legs, a deer lowering its head to graze, a panda sitting down, an aardvark digging, a moose rubbing its antlers on a tree. It is data in the animal's behaviour file, chosen by the sim, and posed by a function of time.

Source: src/animals/archetypes/behaviours.ts, src/sim/animals/animals.ts, src/animals/archetypes/deer.ts, tools/animalSheet.ts

1How an idle works

  1. ListThe behaviour file names its idles: idler([{ state, weight, seconds, needs }]). The state is one of the animal's own states.
  2. ChoiceWhen a standing, awake animal's own timer runs out it draws one by weight, among those whose need is met, and holds it for a random time within its seconds.
  3. TimeThe store carries the state, the seconds since it started and the seconds left.
  4. PoseA script per idle turns those two numbers into the four pose channels. The model decides which parts follow which channel.

Needs. grass means the tile under the animal has a herb layer, tree means a tree stands within 12 tiles. For a tree the animal walks to a spot 1.3 m from the trunk at 0.7 of its pace, turns to face it, and then starts the idle; only the leader of a group does this.

Changing state. Whenever an animal's state changes, an idle starting, ending or cut short by a click, the four pose channels crossfade from what was drawn to what the new state asks for over 0.45 s (PoseBlender in ui/poseBlend.ts, one per animal, smoothstep). The sim only says which state it is in; the scripts need no blend of their own. What switches at once is geometry that depends on the state itself, such as a gait's swing and the aardvark's digging legs.

Not saved, not shown yet in the 3D panel. The idle is picked again from the sim's random stream after a load, and the zoo's 3D panel does not play idles. npm run animal -- <key> --state <idle> --seconds 8 --gif f.gif plays one at its real speed.

2Pose channels

A pose is four numbers. A model part says what each does with a tilt (rotate about a pivot by the channel times a maximum angle, axis: 'yaw' to turn about the vertical) or a point's flex. Channel 3 above 0.6 fades the animal out as a burrow sink, so a yaw keeps below that. Four more pieces of machinery came with the idles:

  • Several tilts on one part (a list, applied in order): the deer's head nods against its neck, and the whole front drops by a second tilt about the hind feet, so the shoulders go down with the neck.
  • Parts that belong to some states (only) and parts that hold still in some states (still, so the aardvark's hind legs stay put while its front legs dig).
  • A driven leg cycle (driven): in a state, one channel is the phase of the walking cycle and another how much of it, so a time script can make the legs scoop without the animal travelling.
  • A drop (drop): a channel lowers the whole animal, so a sitting panda is on the ground and not hovering at hip height.

The brown bear stands on its hind legs, arms hanging and head looking forward. All deer and the moose graze and sniff; the moose also scratches its antlers on a tree with its head lowered, sweeping to one side. The panda sits and breathes. The aardvark digs. The rabbit grooms. The red squirrel eats sitting up with its paws at its mouth, washes its face, and flicks its tail twice (channel 3 is a yaw on the tail about its root). States per animal: aardvark: burrow, dig; rabbit: burrow, groom; reddeer: graze, sniff; panda: sit; brownbear: stand; roedeer: graze, sniff; fallowdeer: graze, sniff; whitetail: graze, sniff; reindeer: graze, sniff; moose: graze, sniff, scratch; squirrel: climb, leap, rear, perch, eat, wash, flick.
