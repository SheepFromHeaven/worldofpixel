Climbing

Every canopy tree is a small graph of trunk and branch nodes, built the same way every time from its species, height and tile, with a load capacity on every limb. An animal has a mass and a few abilities (what trunk it can grip, how far it can touch across, whether it can jump or glide), and it may only use the limbs that hold it and the links its abilities allow. Nothing is stored: a tree's graph is rebuilt on demand and cached.

Source: src/sim/climb/, tools/climbGraph.ts, src/sim/plants/species.ts

1The model

  1. TreeA trunk of nodes every 1.5 m, thinning with height, and branches in the crown, each with a base, a middle and tips and a radius.
  2. StrengthA limb holds wood × 70000 × radius^2.5 kilograms. Wood is a strength factor per species.
  3. AbilityMass, the trunk radius it can grip, how far it touches across, how far it jumps, how it glides.
  4. LinksLimb to limb in one tree. Between trees: touch where crowns meet, jump, or glide down a slope.
  5. RouteA search from the foot of a tree over the nodes the animal may use, cheapest first.

A bear does not jump. The black bear climbs a trunk and out along the thick limbs, and crosses to a neighbour only where two crowns actually touch. Squirrels and martens jump, flying squirrels glide, gibbons swing, the sloth only ever steps across.

Not wired to the animals yet. This is the data and the search. The animals still stand on the ground and the climbers that live in trees are still drawn above the canopy; climbing and leaping as states of the animal comes next.

2How strong a limb is

The strength scales with the 2.5th power of the radius, so a limb twice as thick holds almost six times as much. The wood factor is in the catalogue on the plants page: oak and mahogany 1.3, chestnut and olive 1.2, pine 0.9, birch 0.85, willow 0.55, baobab and kapok 0.5. A landing needs a margin too: an animal that jumps needs the limb it lands on to hold twice its weight (1.25 times for the slow ones).

3A tree as a graph

The crown base, the crown width and the number of branches follow the species: broadleaves branch from about a third of their height and spread widest in the middle, conifers carry short branches from low on the trunk, palms have a trunk and nothing else. Positions come from a hash of the tile, so the same tree is the same tree on every run.
