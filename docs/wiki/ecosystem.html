Ecosystem

Plants are not placed by hand. Each tile gets a growth score from its climate and water, rolls a form from that score, and a species is picked for the form. 45 species are baked into the game. This page shows how a tile gets from numbers to a plant, and gives every species its own curve.

Source: src/sim/eco.ts, src/sim/cover.ts, src/render/groundSprites.ts, src/pixel/ground/plants.ts, src/sim/seasons/

1How a tile gets its plants

  1. EcoA 0 to 255 score from warmth, rain, water table, slope and rock.
  2. CoverPer tile, a roll picks bare, grass, scrub, bush, tree or boulder.
  3. SpeciesA tree tile picks a tree weighted by the biome. Same for bush and boulder.
  4. MarksFlowers, tufts, ferns and stones are scattered by density, in patches.
  5. SeasonEach species answers with what it looks like on this day of the year.

The data is flat arrays. world.eco and world.cover are one byte per tile. They are derived from the field on every load and never saved, except what the player has changed (felled, cleared, trodden). Everything on this page is a pure function of tile and seed, so clearing a tile removes exactly what stood there and the same world always grows the same woods.

2Eco: how well things grow

The score is a product, so one bad factor is enough to stop growth:

eco = warmth0.6 × moisture0.7 × slope × rock × soil

It is zero on water and wherever the warmth is zero. Soil is a noise between 0.7 and 1, a wave 38 tiles across, so equal ground is never perfectly equal.

3Cover: what stands on a tile

Five forms compete for a tile: tree, bush, scrub, boulder and grass. Each biome has an affinity for each form, and each form has a density curve over eco. The affinity scales the curve:

Biometreebushscrubbouldergrass
meadow0.30.80.80.51
forest110.70.60.8
highland0.90.5130.8
marsh0.31.41.40.21
tundra0.150.40.820.7
desert0.10.41.22.50.5

The curves are the same for every biome and only their height changes. A tree wants fertile ground (zero below 0.12, full above 0.55). A bush likes the middle, peaking at 0.6. Scrub peaks at 0.3, on poorer ground. Boulders do better the worse the ground is. Grass needs only a little.

The roll is one number per tile. The tile rolls once, from 0 to 1, and the forms are laid end to end in this order: tree, bush, boulder, scrub, grass. Wherever the roll lands is the cover. Trees and bushes are first, so they take what they want and the later forms get what is left, which is why a woodland has no room for grass.

Trees and bushes are also multiplied by a clump noise 14 tiles across (0.5 to 1.5), so woods come out as woods and not an even scatter. The curves below leave it out, at its average of 1.

4Every species

10 trees, 3 bushes, 4 boulders and 28 ground marks. Sprites are the baked albedo, one per storey for the big ones (low, mid and high stand at different heights and cast different shadows).

Trees, bushes and boulders have two faces: the share bars say how likely this species is to be the one picked when a tile has that form, and the curve multiplies it by the biome's density curve to give the share of all tiles the species holds as eco rises. A line per biome it grows in. Marks are not rolled by tile. Each has a density a tile in the biome it belongs to, multiplied by a patch field (shown as a map, dark where it grows).

5Marks grow in patches

A flat mark's density in a tile is its weight times a patch field (patchiness), then rounded stochastically: a density of 0.4 puts one mark in four tiles. The field is one or more layers of value noise, each with a scale in tiles and a cover, multiplied together and scaled up to keep the mean. Flowers share one big field and each colour has its own inside it. Ferns and mushrooms clump tighter. Cacti and boulders get a wide, loose field and half the density, so they stand apart.

The noise is seeded by the world and is a pure function of the tile, so a patch is the same wherever you come at it from.

6The year

Every species belongs to a seasonal kind. A kind answers one question, stateAt(yearFraction), with an amount for each of leaf loss, autumn colour, blossom, fruit, straw dryness and so on, and how much of the plant is present. Year zero is the first of March. The shaders read a small table built from this each frame, and the northern and southern hemispheres each get their own table, so trees at the equator are a patchwork of autumn and spring.

A deciduous tree is not a thinned crown but a crown that comes off: each oak has a bare twin baked in the same footprint, drawn under the canopy whenever the leaves are going. A flower is not a crown that comes off, so it changes sprite instead: bud, bloom and seed head are three sprites, and a per-flower hash decides which one shows. The bar for 'present' on those charts is the share of flowers showing that sprite.

7What changes the ground

Everything above is the world as generated. Four things rewrite it afterwards, each stamping the 64 tile block it touches so the painter re-bakes that chunk and nothing else: a storm or an uproot turns Cover.Tree into Cover.Fallen (the tile then draws a log with its root plate), a person chopping clears it, a fire clears and treads the tile bare, and walking wears a path. Each tree also carries a trunk sprite drawn under the canopy from the zoom where canopies start to dither away, so as they go the stump they stood on is what shows.
