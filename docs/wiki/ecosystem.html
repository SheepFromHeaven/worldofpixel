Ecosystem

Plants are not placed by hand. Each tile gets a growth score from its climate and water, rolls a form from that score, and a species is picked for the form. Water has its own plants, sorted by depth and current. The species themselves, with every sprite, are on the Plants page.

Source: src/sim/eco.ts, src/sim/aquatic.ts, src/sim/cover.ts, src/render/groundSprites.ts, src/pixel/ground/plants.ts, src/sim/seasons/

1How a tile gets its plants

  1. EcoA 0 to 255 score from warmth, rain, water table, slope and rock.
  2. CoverPer tile, a roll picks bare, grass, scrub, bush, tree or boulder.
  3. SpeciesThe form is only what the tile wants. The species is chosen by niche fitness, and the plant then grows, competes and seeds on the Plants page.
  4. MarksFlowers, tufts, ferns and stones are scattered by density, in patches.
  5. WaterOn water tiles a plant is picked by depth, shore, temperature, salt and the current of the river.
  6. SeasonEach species answers with what it looks like on this day of the year.

The data is flat arrays. world.eco and world.cover are one byte per tile. They are derived from the field on every load and never saved, except what the player has changed (felled, cleared, trodden). Everything on this page is a pure function of tile and seed, so clearing a tile removes exactly what stood there and the same world always grows the same woods.

2Eco: how well things grow

The score is a product, so one bad factor is enough to stop growth:

eco = warmth0.6 × moisture0.7 × slope × rock × soil

It is zero on water and wherever the warmth is zero. Soil is a noise between 0.7 and 1, a wave 38 tiles across, so equal ground is never perfectly equal.

3Cover: what stands on a tile

Generation only. This roll decides what a tile wants (wants) and seeds the first plants. After that plants grow, shade, die and re-seed on their own (see the Plants page), and world.cover is derived from the plants on the tile. Five forms compete for a tile: tree, bush, scrub, boulder and grass. Each biome has an affinity for each form, and each form has a density curve over eco. The affinity scales the curve:

The curves are the same for every biome and only their height changes. A tree wants fertile ground (zero below 0.12, full above 0.55). A bush likes the middle, peaking at 0.6. Scrub peaks at 0.3, on poorer ground. Boulders do better the worse the ground is. Grass needs only a little.

The roll is one number per tile. The tile rolls once, from 0 to 1, and the forms are laid end to end in this order: tree, bush, boulder, scrub, grass. Wherever the roll lands is the cover. Trees and bushes are first, so they take what they want and the later forms get what is left, which is why a woodland has no room for grass.

Trees and bushes are also multiplied by a clump noise 14 tiles across (0.5 to 1.5), so woods come out as woods and not an even scatter. The curves below leave it out, at its average of 1.

Nothing grows on a river. A river tile (world.river: within the ribbon's half width plus a bank of 0.4 tile) rolls no cover at all, so no tree, bush, boulder or grass is generated in the channel, and regrowth skips it too. Flat marks follow the same rule more finely: a mark scattered on a river tile is dropped when its spot is within 0.3 tile of the water the painter draws (wetNear), so the bank keeps its flowers and mushrooms and the water keeps none. Only the water plants below are allowed on the water itself.

4Water plants

Water gets the same treatment as land: a tile's conditions pick the plant. A water tile is one of three things (standing water, a river channel, or the bank beside one), and each plant has a suitability from depth, distance from the shore, temperature, salt and current (suitability in aquatic.ts). The best plant wins the tile, a patch noise per plant decides where it clumps, and a roll against its suitability decides whether anything is there at all. The current of a river tile is the speed the river mesh already draws, from how steeply that reach falls (flowSpeedFor).

The current sorts the plants. In still water reeds, lilies and algae grow, in a slow current only the reeds along the bank, in a moving current the streaming weed, and in rapids nothing holds on. Reeds and lilies need fresh water. In the sea the depth decides: seagrass on the warm shallow shelf, kelp in the colder, deeper water beyond it.

5The year

Every species belongs to a seasonal kind. A kind answers one question, stateAt(yearFraction), with an amount for each of leaf loss, autumn colour, blossom, fruit, straw dryness and so on, and how much of the plant is present. Year zero is the first of March. The shaders read a small table built from this each frame, and the northern and southern hemispheres each get their own table, so trees at the equator are a patchwork of autumn and spring.

A deciduous tree is not a thinned crown but a crown that comes off: each oak, birch, chestnut, apple and willow has a bare twin baked in the same footprint, drawn under the canopy whenever the leaves are going. A flower is not a crown that comes off, so it changes sprite instead: bud, bloom and seed head are three sprites, and a per-flower hash decides which one shows. The bar for 'present' on those charts is the share of flowers showing that sprite.

6What changes the ground

Everything above is the world as generated. Five things rewrite it afterwards, each stamping the 64 tile block it touches so the painter re-bakes that chunk and nothing else: a storm or an uproot turns Cover.Tree into Cover.Fallen (the tile then draws a log with its root plate), a person chopping clears it, a fire clears and treads the tile bare, and walking wears a path. The fifth is that it grows back: plants grow, shade each other, die and re-seed from real parents (Plants, see the Plants page), and a felled tree leaves a fallen log that rots to bare ground, so a hole in a wood fills where the tree stood. Wear fades on the same clock, so a path nobody walks grows over. Each tree also carries a trunk sprite drawn under the canopy from the zoom where canopies start to dither away, so as they go the stump they stood on is what shows.
