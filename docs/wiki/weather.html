Weather

Weather is a state the simulation keeps and the renderer only draws. It lives on a coarse grid of 136 by 96 cells, one per 16 tiles, and is driven by one conductor, Sky, which calls each piece in the order the weather depends on itself. Every map here is a snapshot of that grid from a real run.

Source: src/sim/weather/sky.ts, temperature.ts, winds.ts, clouds.ts, precipitation.ts, groundSurface.ts, stormDamage.ts, lightning.ts

1The conductor

Sky owns the grid and the pieces, and nothing else. Whenever the clock moves, it recomputes the instantaneous state in this order, each step reading only what came before it:

  1. WindsPressure field, then the wind it blows.
  2. TemperatureAir temperature by place, hour and season.
  3. LiftConvergence, slope and heating.
  4. OvercastRead off the cloud water.
  5. Cloud kindsTowering, layered and high.
  6. Surface chillOvercast cools the ground two degrees.
  7. PrecipitationRain, snow, thunder and dust.
  8. GaleFrom wind speed, thunder and dust.

Then, in fixed ticks of 1/240 of a day, with bigger steps after a skip (never dropping time), it moves the things that remember:

  1. Form cloudsMake vapour, condense, rain out, carry on the wind.
  2. GroundWetness and snow cover, integrated.
  3. Storm damageGale, thunder and snow load fell trees.
  4. LightningCharge gathers, discharges, and strikes.

The world is spun up for 36 steps before it opens so there is weather from the first frame. The renderer reads the result as a handful of small textures. The sky panel can force the weather (clear, rain, thunder, snow, storm, sand) by holding the cloud water, the temperature or the dust at a level. A forced weather changes the inputs, so everything downstream still behaves.

The fastest way to go wrong here is to read a number off the wrong step. Temperature is computed before overcast chills it, so "the temperature" is three different arrays: airTemperature, forcedTemperature and surfaceTemperature. Rain and snow read the surface one.

2Temperature

The cell's mean comes from the world field: latitude, less 6.5 degrees a kilometre of altitude (see world generation). On top of it sit two cosines, one for the day and one for the year, each scaled by how continental the place is.

3Pressure and wind

Wind comes from pressure. The pressure of a cell is a zonal base that depends on latitude only, plus moving noise highs and lows, less a thermal term (warm land lowers pressure, cold land raises it). The wind is the gradient, turned. The noise comes in two layers cross-faded every day and restarting on a new seed, because advected for weeks the rows would decorrelate into fine horizontal streaks.

Weather follows pressure: a front is a low, weighted by humidity, so storms sit in lows and the horse latitudes stay dry. Gale is wind speed, from 6 to 18 m/s. The wind map in the game draws the same arrows, with isobars every half unit and the same highs and lows ringed.

4Clouds

Clouds are a conserved quantity the simulation moves, makes and rains out. Each cell keeps vapour and cloud (liquid water). Vapour is made over standing water, wet land and humid land. It condenses when it exceeds a saturation threshold lowered by lift, and re-evaporates if it falls below it. Both are carried on the wind by semi-Lagrangian advection every 1/40 of a day.

Rain is the cloud emptying. Precipitation takes a share of the water every tick (2.6 a day for a heavy cloud), so a raining cloud thins, stops and dissipates unless fed. Sinking air in a high removes cloud and vapour. The kinds of cloud are the renderer's reading of this state, not a second simulation.

5Rain, snow and thunder

Precipitation is read, not simulated: the cloud has already done the work. These three curves were drawn by feeding the real Precipitation class a row of made-up cells.

Dust is the fourth kind: strong wind (from 5 to 11 m/s), dry land and a gust noise, which is what makes a sandstorm in dry windy cells.

6Wet ground and snow

The ground remembers the weather. Every tick it integrates rain, snow and temperature into two numbers per cell, wetness and snowCover, and the renderer lays puddles, darkening and white on the ground from them. The drying rate is about a day, so wet ground outlasts the rain by hours, and snow cover outlasts a cold snap by as long as it stays below freezing.

7Storms, felled trees and lightning

The ground is changed for real. Each tick, a cell rolls a hazard from gale squared, thunder and the weight of settled snow, scaled up by wet soil. It fells that many trees: World.fell turns Cover.Tree into Cover.Fallen and stamps the block so it re-bakes. This is what a world looks like after a long autumn.
